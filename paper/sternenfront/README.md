# Sternenfront

The displayed Clone Wars mode retains the `sternenfront` plugin, server, permission, and storage identities.

## Maps

`BLOCKCRAFT_WORLD_TEMPLATE` lists the maps. Every round plays a fresh copy of a template in a world of its own, which goes
with the round while the server copies the map anew in the background, so no post colour, wall, speeder track or opened
site reaches the next round. The next round follows on the same server, `rounds-in-place` times before it restarts. With
two or more maps the lobby votes with the hotbar's third item. Staff decide with `/forcemap <map>`, where the map is the
template's folder name (`sternenfront`, `sturmwerft`). The vote closes `countdown.vote-closes-at` seconds before the
start, when the chosen map's copy loads and its basin starts loading in the background. With one map listed there is no
vote, and the round plays a copy of it all the same. Without a template the round plays the main world, and the server
restarts after every round. The main world is the first map in the list: the lobby stands there without a shared waiting
island, and `/map save` publishes it.
Wrack von Tessar is built by hand. Sturmwerft, an offshore shipyard in a storm, is generated by `dev/mapgen/sturmwerft.py` and
`dev/verify/verify.py map`; change the script rather than the saved world. A map's `arena.yml` may leave out `secrets` and may
set `weather: rain` and a fixed `time`. On such a map `presentation.lightning` draws harmless strikes near a random fighter, and
real ones are cancelled. Keep its `distance` under spigot.yml's tracking range for other entities, or clients never see them.

Sturmwerft uses broad ocean towers with curved buttresses, ribbed domes, and dark window bands.
Two offshore domes have dark breaching craft with four curved drilling arms each.
Cyan-lit bridges and a warship assembly dock surround the combat deck. Deck scuffs follow the
routes between headquarters and command posts. Maintenance carts, opened machinery, pipes, and replacement panels sit
beside those routes. `environment.steam` and `environment.sparks` in its generated arena metadata locate the ambient
effects. `environment.crane.from` and `to` define the overhead trolley's rail. Its suspended load uses six non-colliding
block displays and is removed at round end. `presentation.shipyard` controls particle counts, range, sound volume, and
crane timing. These effects start with combat and stay off on maps without the corresponding metadata.
Its `world-border-size` includes the offshore scenery while `play-area` keeps the combat and replay bounds.

Both maps have block-built fleets above the battlefield, generated by `dev/mapgen/fleets.py`.
Sturmwerft uses Republic and Separatist ships above the ocean. Tessar uses a smaller formation of the same ship classes.
Their footprints keep the six landing grounds open to the sky.
The ships use credited geometry from SkyblockSquad's Venator and 4LFR3D0zzi's Munificent builds, resampled to 204-block
carriers, 138-block frigates, and 60-block distant ships. Their source links and reuse terms are in `CREDITS.md`.
Sturmwerft's offshore city has 32 main dome towers in several depth layers, with service bridges and satellite pods.
Its 768-block world border contains the city and fleet. Tessar uses a 512-block border for its larger fleet.
Sturmwerft's `environment.sea` supports emit moving foam crests and spray, with positional rough-surf audio and wind.
`presentation.sea` tunes these effects. They use loaded chunks near viewers and leave the water blocks unchanged.

Each entry under `secrets` is optional. Tessar keeps `crystals`, `droid-path`, `dune`, `music-box`, and `course`.
A shipyard can supply only `receiver: "x y z"` or `inspection-course`. The latter has `lane` as
`[minX, minY, minZ, maxX, maxY, maxZ]`, `pit-y` as a number, `gates` as ordered X planes from start to finish,
and `board: "x y z"`. Gates can run west to east or east to west. The inspection course uses fixed physical platforms;
the moving crane's displays provide no collision.

The receiver consumes right clicks before weapons, plays the original cantina tune and grants `sternenfront.receiver`
once through `Discoveries` and `UnlockRewards`. Its German transmission appears in chat so the combat HUD cannot replace it.
Only deployed players on foot can activate it.
The inspection course reuses `KesselRun` and grants `sternenfront.inspection` on the first valid completion.
`secrets.inspection-course.par-seconds` sets its timing goal; `limit-seconds` ends an attempt.
Its board shows the five fastest times in the current round and never reads or writes `bc_sternenfront_kessel`.
Mounting, teleporting, death, leaving the lane or falling below `pit-y` interrupts an attempt.
Round end and plugin disable remove secret displays and stop playback and tasks.

## Field objectives

A map can declare circuits in `blockcraft/Sternenfront/field-objectives.yml`. The [example](field-objectives.example.yml) shows a shield generator, power relay, and communications console linked to one circuit. Maps without this file keep their existing rules.

Each circuit has an owning faction and lists of door and light placement IDs from the map's scenery files. Devices name their circuit and position. Shield generators also list the exact air cells used for their native glass shield. Keep these cells clear of structures and door passages.

Sneak and right-click a device to begin an interaction. Hold sneak within four blocks until the channel completes. Damage, leaving the field, moving out of range, or releasing sneak cancels it. `field-objectives.channel-seconds` controls the duration.

- An owner repairs a relay. An enemy disables it. Loss of power switches linked lights off, opens linked doors, and removes the circuit's shields.
- An enemy console interaction overrides security for `field-objectives.override-seconds`. Doors open and shields turn off while the lights retain power. An owner clears the override.
- An owner restores a shield generator. An enemy disables it. A restored generator still requires a powered circuit without an active override.

The models show active and disabled states. Interaction progress stays in the combat HUD, so starting or cancelling a channel keeps the furniture in place. Damaged variants remain separate assets for authored damage states. These interactions award no points. Native shield cells block movement and shots. A door refuses to close while a living entity occupies its passage.

`FieldObjectives` owns match behavior and restores its devices, shields, and linked fixture states at cleanup. The shared `FurnitureSolids` service owns physical collision and world illumination. The lobby uses the same models without match controllers.

## Compatibility

`kolonien` identifies the Republic and `hegemonie` the Separatists in arena metadata, configuration, and player
settings. The hero keys `jedi`, `commander`, `sith`, and `bounty-hunter` identify Obi-Wan, Rex, Dooku, and Jango.
Changing display names does not rename those persisted keys.

## Soldier weapons and reference models

Republic soldiers can choose the EE-3, A280, or E-11D. Click the weapon in the waiting-lobby class menu, or right-click the soldier card when redeploying. The choice lasts for the round and survives class changes. Droid loadouts retain their weapons.

The A280 fires three bolts per click; the E-11D fires automatically. Their damage, heat, range, and timing live in `blasters.a280` and `blasters.e11d`. All three use the existing aim, recoil, heat, and replay handling.

`SternenfrontArt.java` authors the reference rifles, Katarn commando armor, updated 181st clone markings, B2 plates, and Dooku cape. `SternenfrontGallery.py` generates 69 exhibition entries, including 15 complete characters rendered with one item display each. Run `:resourcepack:generateSternenfrontGallery` to regenerate them.

## Reinforcement units

The deploy menu offers reinforcements bought with battle points for one life. Right-click the Republic reinforcement card to switch between ARC trooper and clone commando, then left-click to select it.
The Republic's ARC trooper costs 250 points, has 32 health, and carries a cooler-firing support repeater with shield wall and adrenaline.
The Separatists' BX commando droid costs 300 points, has 26 health and 112% movement speed, and carries a short-range carbine with jump pack and grenade.
The clone commando costs 300 points, has 32 health, and carries a DC-17m with shield wall and grenade.
Each reinforcement type has two slots alongside the named heroes. `reinforcement-units` configures each unit's cost, limit, health, and speed.
`blasters.arc-repeater`, `blasters.commando-carbine`, and `blasters.dc17m` configure their weapons. The abilities use the existing `abilities` settings.

Selection reserves nothing. Choosing a spawn confirms the purchase; changing the paid role requires confirmation again.
Automatic deployment without confirmation uses the free class. Deployment checks the balance and capacity again, and a failed deployment restores the points and slot.
If the unit is unavailable, the player deploys as their free class. Death, disconnect, and round end release the unit.
Taking the relic also releases the unit and replaces its loadout with the champion's. Reinforcements receive normal healing and normal kill rewards.
They have no permanent unlock or database record. Their body rigs and helmets come from `resourcepack/SternenfrontArt.java`.

## Bots

Bots fill each side to `bots.fill-per-team` fighters, players included, and fight in fireteams of up to
`bots.squad.fireteam-size`, led by a soldier where the team has one. Each new bot takes the class its side has fewest of.
A bot deploys where a player of its side could, at headquarters or a held post, whichever lies nearest its fireteam's
objective. Within a spawn, bots and players land on the spot whose nearest enemy is farthest off, counted in four-block
steps up to 24 blocks. Among those they take one with nobody within three blocks, and a spot handed out in the last
three seconds counts as taken. The rest is chance, so nobody lands on the same spot every life.
A team shares one objective: a post it can still take, or a held post with enemies in it, keeping its choice unless another
is `switch-margin` blocks closer. A held post under attack counts `reinforce-pull` blocks closer, enough to bring a team
back from the neighbouring post but not across the map. When a team moves on from a post it took, it leaves its heavy,
else a soldier or scout other than its leader, as the post's guard; it keeps one guard out at most. The guard moves
between the post's lookouts and rejoins the team when the post falls. Once every post is held, teams hold the one nearest
the enemy. A team at a post its side holds keeps off the capture point, which only a guard holds: it pushes attackers it
knows of, or enemies standing in the post, and otherwise takes the post's lookouts nearest the enemy, facing them. A team near a player of its faction takes that player's order, unless that player has stayed within three blocks of one
spot and fired no shot for `idle-ticks`. A team coming from
another post joins the new route nearest its inner end rather than walking back to its outer waypoints.

On the move the others walk in the leader's steps, `spacing` blocks apart, each `stagger` blocks to alternate sides where the
ground allows. A member keeps the leader's pace once in its place and runs only when it falls a few places behind. The
leader slows for a fit member behind it, and a member ahead waits for the column. Under fire the team bounds
by pairs along its authored route: one pair runs `bound-distance` blocks while the other covers it from cover and fires at
where the enemy was last seen. The pairs alternate over the members near the leader that neither guard nor flank, and
the pair without the leader runs first. A team held up for `flank-after-ticks` sends one member round by another route. Near a
post it is taking the whole team spreads into it. Bots take cover only while covering, venting or wounded. A covering
bot, or one waiting for its column, that sees an enemy and finds no cover steps two or three blocks to alternating sides
of its line to that enemy.

A bot below 30% health leaves the push until it heals past that: it takes cover, else runs to its fireteam's medic, else
falls back from the enemy, else stands still. A wounded leader hands the lead to a fit member. Bots regenerate out of
combat by the same `regen` rule as players, and medics heal bots as they heal players.

A team whose leader has twice as many enemies as friends within `fall-back-range`, and at least two, runs back to its
route's retreat points. It pushes again as soon as it is back there and no longer outnumbered, and waits there
`fall-back-ticks` at most. Odds still bad by then, or bad again within `fall-back-ticks` of pushing, make it give the post
up for another objective. A leader left alone in a fight falls back once the same way to wait for its teammates. A guard
whose post is that outnumbered rejoins its team, and a guard that stays takes cover first when it is wounded or venting.
A bot that stands within 1.5 blocks of one spot for `stand-ticks` while it knows where an enemy is runs there for five
seconds: an enemy it sees, one it saw recently, or its team's contact under fire, never one hidden behind a wall that nobody
spotted. A press that leaves the bot where it stood gives up until it moves again. Wounded bots, medics treating a patient, bots falling back, heavies in siege mode and guards at a lookout stay put.
When a member falls, its teammates take the killer's position as contact for
`alarm-ticks`; a bot with nothing in sight turns toward enemy fire within `hearing-range`. Soldiers and heavies throw their
explosives at enemies dug into a post or hidden behind cover, and a heavy holding open ground under fire raises its shield
wall. A bot's aim scatters on a fresh target and settles over `aim-settle-ticks`, and its first shots go to one side
of the target, closing in over the same ticks. It opens up while the bot walks and as its blaster heats, by the same
`combat.handling` rule as a player's. It aims `aim-lead` of the way ahead of a moving target that its bolt needs, at
the chest where it can see it and just under the eyes where a wall hides the chest, and fires only once its aim is within
8 degrees of that point. A bot with a long rifle sees `bots.marksman.sight-range` blocks and aims with `aim-share` of its
profile's `aim-spread`. A hit and gunfire throw a bot off as they throw a player off: a body hit undoes
`bots.shaken.hit-flinch` of its settling, and each enemy bolt that passes within the near-miss radius of its eyes
suppresses it, which widens its spread until it fades. Each member watches its own sector.

A bot turns on whoever shoots it. An enemy that hit it within `memory-ticks` counts as half as far away when it picks a
target, and it sees that enemy behind it too and as far as its own blaster reaches; turning on it takes half the usual
reaction time. A bot forgets a target at once when that target dies or leaves the field.

## Arena template

A map's template is restored before each server start. Positions and bot routes come from
`blockcraft/Sternenfront/arena.yml` inside that template. A map edit needs both the saved blocks and updated metadata.

- A post's listed colour blocks must remain recolourable. The round replaces their material for the current owner.
- Door panels start as air. The round opens any panels left closed in a saved template.
- Landing grounds lie at x -22 and x 22 beside each post. Each needs a flat 7-by-7 surface, open sky, and 6.5 blocks
  of clearance around its centre for the assault craft.
- A vehicle two blocks wide that steps one block must reach every landing ground from the headquarters pads.
- Replays include the play area. The far mesas outside it are scenery and are absent from a replay.

## Client constraints

Body rigs are cosmetic. Combat uses the underlying fighter's hitbox, including the larger-looking B2. Fighters see
other deployed bodies as rigs; the owner keeps their normal first-person and F5 body. Spectators see the original
entities of rigged bodies so the kill camera remains usable, and the plain model of animated bots. Hiding a player's body
also removes them from that viewer's tab list.

A recording holds the bare fighters. `RecordedModel` marks a bot with its model, so a replay server with BetterModel draws
and animates it again; without the plugin it plays back as an equipped skeleton. Players play back as mannequins.

Left click fires and right click aims. A blaster's `mode` in `blasters` is `automatic` or `single`: an automatic one
(the carbines, the repeaters, the heavy rifle) fires for as long as left click is held, and a single one (the pistols and
the long rifles) fires once for each click. A click up to three ticks before the blaster is ready fires once it is, and a
held trigger fires again by itself after a vent.

The 26.3 client sends a left click once when it goes down and never again while it is held, except while it breaks a
block: then it swings every tick and sends an abort when the button comes up. `TriggerCells` gives a player with an
automatic blaster such blocks, which only their client has: unlit `light` blocks in the air cells around their eyes. A
light block has a shape only for someone holding a `light` item, has no collision, draws nothing and cannot be broken.
So a player's blaster is a `light` item with the hoe's `item_model`, the pack's models unchanged, and `can_break` for
light. With the eyes inside a cell every look meets it first, so the click starts breaking it, and `Trigger` reads the
start, the swings and the abort as a pull, a hold and a release. Neither the spear's `piercing_weapon` nor its
`kinetic_weapon`, `attack_range`, `minimum_attack_charge` or `swing_animation` repeats a held click: a piercing weapon's
jab reaches the server as a `STAB` action that Paper turns into no event, and a kinetic weapon charges while right click
is held.

What this costs, and what keeps it small:

- The client outlines a block it may break, here the cell around the player's head. Each Sternenfront map carries the
  datapack `datapacks/sternenfront`, which sets the overworld's `fog_end_distance` to 65536, further than any client
  draws, so nothing looks different. The pack's `rendertype_lines.vsh` reads that distance as "this is Sternenfront" and
  draws no block outline there, in first and third person, and every outline in every other game. Fog of another kind
  has its own end, so the outline shows while the camera is under water.
- The client plays `block.stone.hit` each time it begins breaking a cell: on a pull, when the eyes cross into the next
  cell, and when the held item changes. The server cuts the sound short as soon as it hears of the start, and nothing
  can mute it for a cell alone. A player's own item therefore changes as rarely as it can (see the vents below), and a
  venting gun has no cells, so a trigger held through a vent is silent.
- The client sends no use while it breaks a block, so the server cannot see a player who holds the trigger begin or
  stop aiming. Every ten ticks of a held trigger, right after a shot, the cells go away for one tick: the client stops
  breaking, sends a use if right click is down and starts again before the next shot is due. A right click that goes
  down or comes up in the middle of a burst therefore counts within about half a second, and until then the aim the
  hold began with carries on.
- The server answers a use on a cell with the real blocks there. An aiming player has no cells for that reason: their
  first shot is a plain click, the cells come back with it, and the hold is read from the swings until the client
  starts breaking one. Battlefield controls and secrets take the block the player looks at through a cell.
- Cells are sent only once the pack is applied, and never for a single-shot blaster, which needs none.

Paper fires `PlayerArmSwingEvent` once for each swing, on air, a block or an entity, and a click on a fighter close by
with a blaster in hand fires instead of punching. A blaster's `attack_animation` is none, so the click does not swing
the gun out of view, and the swings of a held trigger are cancelled so nobody else sees them. The client repeats a held
right click every four ticks, which the server reads as aiming until the next would have arrived, and the item's model
sees the client's own use key. The client narrows its view only from a slower walk and only with FOV effects on, so
aiming zooms only for those clients.

A long rifle is a hoe like every other blaster. A spyglass would zoom tenfold whatever the settings, but the client
drops every click while an item is in use, so it could fire only when right click was let go. While the rifle aims, the
helmet carries the reticle, `blockcraft:misc/long_rifle_scope`, as its `equippable` camera overlay, which only the
wearer sees. The client draws `(movement speed / walking speed + 1) / 2` of its field of view and reads the abilities'
walking speed for nothing else, so the scope tells it a negative walking speed that leaves `aim.scope-zoom` of the view:
0.25, a fourfold zoom at full FOV effects. Paper's `setWalkSpeed` also sets the movement speed's base, which the scope
puts back at once. The walking speed reaches the client at once, while the server sends the movement speed with the
player's entity data every other tick, so the scope flips the silent flag there and back to send both in one tick;
otherwise the zoom would jump or stall when the scoped walk slows or frees the player. The client keeps at least a tenth
of its view. Nothing else sets a negative walk speed, so it marks a raised scope, and joining the game clears one that
a crash saved with the player.
Music and cantina audio use the Music category. Combat effects use the Players category.

Launched rockets and orbital strikes continue after their owner falls. Lightning channels end with the caster.
Both powers use the existing damage path for spawn protection and kill credit. Rockets also use `blastAround` for
cover, teammate safety, and self-damage. Lightning uses the saber guard without reflecting the attack.

## Redeployment

Clicking a spawn chooses and confirms it in one step with the selected class, hero or reinforcement: the player deploys
there at once, or as soon as the five-second wait ends. The yellow button under the map does the same for the suggested
spawn. Without confirmation, the fifteen-second timer deploys the existing free class there without
spending battle points. Selecting a different paid unit, or going back from one to a class, drops the confirmation,
including when a spawn was already queued, so the spawn needs another click. A failed teleport also drops it and restarts
the automatic countdown, leaving the menu open for another choice. The small red cross at the right of the strip above
the cards, `Schließen` on hover, closes the menu; the countdown goes on, the deploy item opens it again, and the menu
stays closed until then.

The suggestion keeps an explicitly chosen spawn for the current deployment if it is still safe. Outside an assault,
it remembers a safe post selected by clicking its spawn card. HQ is never a lasting preference, and the assault priority
takes precedence over remembered posts. Otherwise it prefers a safe owned post closer to the objective, with HQ as the fallback.
Quick deployment does not save its suggestion as a preference. The actual deployment rechecks ownership
and live enemy presence after the fade; a lost or threatened post is replaced by another safe recommendation.
A post also needs a spawn at least `respawn.enemy-clearance` blocks from every enemy, by default 12 blocks.
The dropship remains an explicit choice and is never recommended automatically.

The deploy menu is one screen. The pack draws a window wider and taller than the chest around it, from
`resourcepack/DeployArt.java`, sized for the smallest automatic GUI, 426 by 240 pixels, which Minecraft's default window and
720p at scale 3 get. Left of the slots it shows the chosen class, reinforcement or hero: portrait, health, pace, damage
and range, each beside its own symbol (a heart, a boot, a blaster and a crosshair, which the cards' tooltips share),
a dash where a hero's blade has none, weapon, both abilities with their keys, the cost and the player's
battle points as `Deine Kampfpunkte`. Right of them it lists the spawns with the marks their markers wear and explains
the markers. In the chest are the close cross, the four class cards, then the reinforcement and both heroes
with their costs, in red and with a padlock while the player cannot pay or no slot is free. The chest's title draws that
window, so it cannot change while the chest is open. A click on a class, a reinforcement, a hero or a spawn, a change in
battle points or in what the player can pay for, or the dropship coming or going opens the menu again with a new one at
once. A spawn locking, opening or becoming the recommendation must first hold for `respawn.screen-settle-ticks`, by
default two and a half seconds, because an enemy walking along a post's clearance would otherwise reopen the window
again and again, and a click while it reopens is lost. Its marker shows the change at once. The countdowns and the
posts' blinking are items and change in place.

The line under the map says how to choose. While a paid reinforcement or hero is selected and affordable, it names the
pick and its cost, and every open spawn's marker says what a click there costs. A click on a locked spawn plays the deny
sound and puts the reason in that line in red until the next click: an enemy or neutral post, enemies too close to an
own post, or the dropship outside the trailing side's assault. The window covers the action bar and the chat, so the
reason goes on the screen itself. From the fade to the deployment on, the menu takes no more choices, so the player
lands where and as whom they were when it began. A card plays only the sound of its outcome, and a locked spawn only the deny sound.

The spawns stand on a map in the player's own inventory, which the menu borrows while it is open: north up, east right,
the current map's posts and headquarters at one scale, overlapping markers moved apart. The dropship has the bottom-right
cell. While a dead player chooses, that inventory holds only the deploy item. The menu gives each slot back whatever
closes it, when it closes, the player dies, quits, is kicked or the server stops, unless something else changed the slot
meanwhile, as deploying does. Each item the menu lays there carries the one it replaced, so when a crash saved the
menu's items instead, the player's next join puts their own back. The vanilla `Inventory` label falls on a band in its own grey, so it does not show.

Posts A–E use the same letter badges as the combat HUD. Filled badges allow deployment, outlines mark locked posts,
and blinking badges mark contested or attacked posts. Colours show ownership. The pack frames the chosen spawn on its
slot's border: gold corners while it waits for a confirmation, a green frame and a glow once confirmed. A star marks the
recommended spawn and a padlock a locked one; the list beside the map repeats the star, the frame and the padlock. The
deploy button spans the hotbar under the map and carries the countdown: `Einsetzen in 4 s` during the wait, `Jetzt
einsetzen · auto in 9 s` once the wait is over, with the seconds to the automatic deployment, and `Einsatz in 4 s` once
confirmed. Its words change seldom and are part of the title, which reopens when the wait ends; the seconds
are an item in one of its slots, which picks the slot that centres the line best. Hovering the button names its target.
Hovering a marker shows the post name, recommendation, selection, and for a locked spawn `Gesperrt` with the reason.
The dropship names the post it lands beside, and its cell stays empty while there is none.

### Landing

A deployment at headquarters or a post lands the player from their side's craft once the fade turns black: the Republic
gunship slides its side doors aft and the Separatist lander folds its hatches down into ramps, both from
`resourcepack/SternenfrontArt.java`. The player rides in its bay facing the open door, the craft comes down beside the
spot, and its door opens onto it. The craft sets them down on the spot, or over stalls and walls up to six blocks high it
hovers and drops them, and a drop does not hurt. Their look turns as far as the door was from the spot's facing, so
looking out of the door leaves them facing the way the spot does. The craft tries the door towards the spot's facing
first, then the sides, then the far side. Under a roof it comes straight down from just below the roof. Where there is no
room for that, and at the assault's dropship, the plain fade stays. Teammates who deploy at the same spawn within
`share-ticks` ride along and step out beside each other. Bots keep the plain deployment.

`presentation.landing` in `config.yml` switches it off and sets its height, glide and timing. The ride is part of the
deployment: the rider stays hidden and unhurt, the menu takes no choices, and the spawn's lock is checked again when the
craft sets them down, as after the fade. A rider leaves only when the craft sets them down: sneaking does nothing. Dying
or quitting removes their craft at once if no one else rides in it, and the round's end removes every craft. The craft is display entities
and blocks nobody. It comes down in one move that the client draws, because Grim counts every teleport of a vehicle
under a rider as a refused teleport. For the same reason riders leave the vehicle a tick before they are set down.

After spawning, the minimap frames the post to make for in yellow corners: the assault's priority post, or the nearest
objective outside the assault. When all posts are safe, it frames the post nearest the enemy HQ for defence. The top
line frames the assault's priority post in the same corners. The first deployment of a round ends its chat lines with
one that names the yellow corners as the player's target. The star stays the deploy menu's recommended spawn.

## Minimap

A deployed player with the pack sees a map in the top-left corner, north up and centred on them, `minimap.radius` blocks
to its rim. It shows the posts with the combat HUD's letter badges and ownership colours, both headquarters, the player's
arrow at the nearest of sixteen headings, their squad as gold dots and other allies as smaller dots of their side's colour.
Posts beyond the radius stay on the rim along their bearing. Yellow corners frame the post to make for, as Redeployment describes. Headquarters and fighters beyond it are left out, so
nothing on the rim hides a post's badge. An enemy shows for `minimap.enemy-ticks` after each shot it fires, and only
while the player's client draws it, so a vanished player or a hidden body never shows. The list under the map, headed by
`minimap.squad`, names the player and their squad in the menu tiles' capitals and dims anyone dead or choosing a spawn.
Each name follows a mark of what the fighter carries: a rifle for a soldier, a shield for a heavy, a crosshair for a scout,
a cross for a medic and a crown for a hero. `minimap.bot` in `messages.yml` names the bots.

A player's squad is the fireteam that last took their order (see Bots). It stays theirs while no other team takes it,
and passes to the next team that does once it follows someone else or nobody. Other players with the same squad are listed.

The map is a boss bar line of its own that the pack's text shader moves into the corner, and it empties with the combat
HUD during a scene and while the player is dead. The line itself stays for as long as a player
with the pack is in the round, so the bars keep one order and never jump a slot: the objective line, the map's, then a
post's capture bar and the heroes' bars. A bar that arrives late, such as the objective line for a player who rejoins,
puts the ones after it back below it. The map redraws every `minimap.update-ticks` and sends nothing while nothing on it
moved.

The map's line also carries the round's short announcements as its own centred text, at chat size just under the
objective, where a subtitle would cross the crosshair: the spawn's name on arrival, a post secured, the relic appearing,
an act beginning and a secret found. Each stays a few seconds, a new one replaces it, and it hides with the combat HUD.
A player without the line, such as a spectator, gets the same text as a subtitle. The subtitle itself carries only the
crosshair's marks.

## Combat feedback

During combat, enemies have red outlines with every weapon, without aiming or sneaking. This applies to players and
bots, including their custom body parts. Outlines are private to the viewer. `outlines.enabled`, `outlines.range` and
`outlines.allies`, which adds cyan outlines for allies, control them. The applied resource pack clips outline pixels against the
world depth buffer, so walls hide the covered parts while exposed parts stay outlined. Selection uses no server-side
block rays. Outlines stay disabled until the pack has loaded.

Each shot's sound is pitched up to 6% higher or lower at random. A player's shots carry 32 blocks and a bot's 24. The
shooter hears their own shot at full volume, and it follows them as they move. A bolt that hits a fighter or a wall
plays an impact sound. A shooter's own bolt first shows four blocks ahead of their eyes, in a small glow, and leaves
smaller glowing dots (`presentation.bolt.own-trail-size`) than the trail everyone else sees, because from behind every
dot lies on the crosshair. A repeater (the Z-6, the B2's wrist blaster and the ARC trooper's support blaster) also
thumps with every bolt and kicks its shooter's view up for a tick by `presentation.heavy-blaster.kick-degrees`. A
relative turn of the view needs no teleport, so the kick holds no movement. Its bolts land with a blast sound and burst
in sparks and smoke, which nobody within four blocks gets.
A hit body flashes `appearance.hurt-tint` for ten ticks, and an animated bot body also flinches.
A hit bot clanks if it is a droid and grunts if it is a clone, at most once per `bots.hurt-sound.cooldown-ticks`.

An enemy bolt, a bot's included, that passes within `combat-feedback.near-miss.radius` blocks of a player's eyes without
hitting them whizzes past from its nearest point, louder and higher the nearer it came. Each pass also darkens the edges
of the screen in coarse dithered grey pixels, further in the more bolts pass, and the darkness fades within a second. It
keeps clear of the middle, so the crosshair and the target stay in sight, and it is grey where a hit's edges are red.
A bolt that hits pushes its victim, bots included, a little along its flight, by `combat.hit-push` and in place of the
vanilla knockback. Nobody in the air is pushed, nor anyone towards an edge with no ground beyond the push.
A headshot rings the victim's helmet, tilts their view back once after the hit's own tilt and shakes the picture with
split colours for `combat-feedback.headshot-rattle.ticks`. A forced turn of the view would ride on a teleport, which
holds the player's movement until their client confirms it, so the rotation and with it the aim stay the player's own.
A headshot bot's aim scatters again as on a fresh target, and a body hit undoes part of its settling.

A long rifle's scope glints (`presentation.scope-glint`): while a player looks through one, and while a bot with one has
an enemy in its sights, a small pixel star flares at the scope every `interval-ticks` for each enemy player within
`range` who is in front of the scope, sees it within `view-degrees` of their look and has no wall in between. Each such
viewer gets a display of their own, sized by distance so it stays a few pixels across.

Hits, headshots, and kills have distinct sounds and immediate marks around the crosshair; a headshot's has a diamond above it. A kill replaces a weaker
marker even within the same tick. A red arc around the crosshair points at whoever last hurt the player, for
`combat.damage-indicator-ticks`, and follows as they turn. While a player with the pack holds a blaster that has spread,
four ticks around the crosshair show it. They open one step each time the spread, worked out as for the next shot, passes
a multiple of that blaster's base in `combat-feedback.spread-steps`, so every blaster closes fully at the sights, one
step wider standing still, and opens fully hot on the move. A scoped long rifle shows none, and the vanilla crosshair stays inside
the ticks. All three share the title's subtitle, centred with `UiFont`.
Kill and assist popups appear briefly beside the heat or guard meter.
A player with the pack sees their own kills, assists and death in the top-right corner, newest on top, at most four
lines. A line reads killer, mark and victim in the minimap's capitals, with the player's own name in white and others
in their side's colour: a skull for a kill or a death, its outline for an assist and a diamond beside it for a headshot.
Bots go by their minimap call signs. A line greys out in its last second and is gone after `kill-feed.seconds`. It rides
on the minimap's boss bar line, and stays up while the player is dead so the kill cam shows who it was. Sternenfront's
own potion effects hide their icons, which would otherwise cover the feed.
A grenade holds `abilities.grenade.charges` throws, full on each deployment, and they come back one at a time, each
`cooldown-seconds` after the one before. The HUD counts the throws left and shows the seconds to the next only when none is
left, which is also the only time the vanilla cooldown runs on its material. Bots keep a plain cooldown.
Hero and elite availability appear only for `combat-feedback.hero-ready-ticks` and `combat-feedback.elite-ready-ticks`;
idle kill counts and point progress stay hidden.
Nearby gains combine into one segment.
A player with the pack sees their abilities as a column of tiles in the screen's bottom-right corner: F against the
corner, Q above it, then a bladed hero's leap under a badge with the space bar twice, and a kill streak's strike framed in gold under
Shift+F until it is thrown. A key with nothing bound shows no tile. Each tile carries its key on a badge and the
ability's full name beside it. The names stand level with the hotbar and the food bar, so on a GUI narrower than 380
pixels, such as a 4:3 or 5:4 window at its automatic scale, the pack's shader leaves them out and the tiles stay. A ready tile is bright and framed in the side's colour; a recharging one turns grey
behind the seconds left and a fill that rises as it recharges. An ability that holds several uses counts those left in
its tile's bottom-right corner, as the hotbar counts a stack, and shows no count once it is empty. The tiles ride
on the action bar: `CombatHud` puts them in the line's middle, which needs the line's width as the client measures it,
and the pack's text shader moves them into the corner. Feedback that replaces the action bar, such as an ability's
cooldown notice, hides them until it ends. `Kühlt` means cooling. A loadout change
explains each binding in chat with its key, full name and what it does, after a line on the weapon: a blaster's fire
and aim, a long rifle's scope, or a blade's swing and guard. Chat, the round's opening line and the class cards name the
keys the player bound to sneaking, swapping hands and dropping, which the client fills in. The badges and the deploy
screen always show F and Q: their text's width is counted, and a binding's name, such as `Maustaste 4`, has a width
only the client knows and does not fit a badge.
`ObjectiveHud` shows a player in the round their own reinforcements after `Wir`, a badge per post, the enemy's
reinforcements and the time left in a boss bar's text line, and the pack draws that colour and overlay of bar as nothing.
A spectator has no side to call `Wir` and sees no such line. The screen's
edges redden with a hit and while health is low. `combat.handling` tunes spread and damage falloff.

Holding right click with a blaster aims it, and sneaking does not. In first person the gun swaps to its `_aim` model
the moment the button goes down, with the top of its sights just under the crosshair, and kicks back there with a shot.
The spread multiplies by `combat.handling`'s `aiming`, and left click fires. Aiming slows the walk a little, to
`2 * aim.zoom - 1` of its pace, which a client with FOV effects on draws as a view narrowed to `aim.zoom`; without FOV
effects the view keeps its width. The server learns of the release only when the next repeat fails to come, so the
slower walk and tighter spread last up to six ticks after it.
A long rifle aims the same way and looks through its scope while it does: the reticle covers the screen, the walk drops
to `aim.scoped-walk`, and with FOV effects on the view narrows to `aim.scope-zoom`. Left click fires, in the scope or
from the hip. In the scope the spread shrinks to nothing over `settle-ticks`, after which a shot goes exactly where the
reticle points. Other viewers see the rifle at the sights like any blaster. Bots have no scope.
Spread opens on the move and more in the air, and `combat.handling.blasters` sets those factors per blaster, so pistols
stay steadier walking and the repeater wants aiming. A sprint shows the lowered pose of the held blaster. A pull of the
trigger or a right click brings the gun up out of the sprint: it fires or aims `sprint-recovery-ticks` later, and the
player moves at a walk until twelve ticks after the last shot or aim. Only the client can end its sprint, so the server
takes the sprint's pace away instead. The crosshair ticks use the same factors as the shot.
A title's subtitle stays readable under the marks, which `UiFont.centredOn` keeps on the crosshair however long the line is. The sidebar stays hidden during a fight.

A flame and a muted `Hitze` name the heat meter; a bladed hero's guard meter has a shield and `Deckung` instead.
The heat meter turns red and shows `HEISS` at `heat.warning-level`, or when the next shot would overheat the weapon.
Venting shows `Kühlt` until the weapon is ready, followed by a ready sound. A vent also brackets `heat.window-share` of the
meter in cyan, somewhere in its lower half, and the meter then lights every cell the cooling heat has not left. The first
fresh pull of the trigger while the last lit cell is inside the bracket ends the vent with a click and steam; one outside
it adds `heat.miss-penalty-share` of the vent and closes the window. A click less than eight ticks after the overheating
shot or the click before counts for neither, so clicking straight through a vent catches nothing, and bots never try.
`combat-feedback` tunes marker and
popup durations and the hit, headshot, and kill volumes.
The vents on every blaster show its heat to its holder and everyone else: grey when cold, orange from `heat.glow.warm`,
bright from `heat.warning-level`, and pulsing while venting. The colour is the held item's first custom_model_data
colour, so the item changes only when the step does. The client does not lower the gun for these changes, because the
item keeps its type and its override sets `hand_animation_on_swap` to false. A player's own item never takes the firing
pose and does not pulse while they hold the trigger, since each change would make their client start breaking its
trigger cell again. Their own view kicks from a three-tick cooldown on the item instead, which the client counts down
itself and the model reads as `minecraft:cooldown`, and everyone else is sent the kicked, pulsing item as that player's
equipment. Animated bot bodies leave the vents out, because their rig has no item to tint them; the display bodies show
them.

A player's bolts and long-rifle shots meet fighters, speeders and heads where that player's screen showed them, which is
their ping plus the client's smoothing of other entities behind the server. Each tick records every target's hitbox for that.
Walls, shields, guard and parry stay in the present, and bots' shots see only the present. `combat.lag-compensation` turns
it off or caps how far back it reaches.

Left click sweeps a saber-colored slash through every enemy ahead, including when aimed at air. Colored particles
follow a continuous white line with a solid core visible from the side. Wall-clipped hit rays position its segments,
each lasting `presentation.saber-trail-ticks` under the shared effect cap. Each enemy can take damage or block only once per swing.
`combat.melee` sets the reach, arc angle, cooldown and animation duration. Defaults are 3.5 blocks, 120 degrees,
12 ticks between swings and a four-tick sweep. Successive swings alternate direction. Switching weapons or guarding
cancels the remaining sweep. The relic blade uses the same attack.
A saber's hum, ignition, retraction and swings and the raised guard play on the hero, so they follow them as they move.
A viewer who sees the hero's body rig hears them on its torso, because a client drops a sound on an entity it does not
draw. The thrown saber flies flat and spins about its middle like a disc, whirling every five ticks.
Other fighters see a bladed hero's body rig hold the blade tipped forward, bring both hands in with the blade across the
chest while guarding, wind up on the side a swing starts from and follow through over the sweep, and thrust the off hand
forward while a lightning channel or grip lasts. A Force push first draws that hand back for `wind-up-ticks` while air
gathers at the palm and a rising whoosh plays, then thrusts it forward as the push lands. Force lightning leaves that
hand as forked bolts redrawn every tick in a new shape every second tick, while its damage still lands on the channel's
pulses. Display interpolation eases each change over a few ticks. In first person the guard rolls the blade across the
view through the item's `using_item` model.

## What keeps a player going

Within a life, kills chain: each one within `combat-feedback.multikill-seconds` of the last adds `heroes.points.multikill` and a
higher chime, and a killing headshot adds `heroes.points.headshot`. Both scale with what the kill pays, so bots pay less than
players. A bot kill counts towards stats, pays `rewards.bot-kill` and fills the orbital strike's streak at `streaks.bots-per-kill`.
A kill by something still flying after its owner fell pays as usual but does not count towards the next life's streak.

Battle points also feed the round's awards, which need two fighters and pay `rewards.award`, and a rank that lasts across rounds:
`bc_sternenfront_career` keeps every player's total and best round, `career.ranks` sets the thresholds and `career.rank.<index>`
in `messages.yml` names them. The details button of the round recap in chat adds battle points and captured posts to the
shared results and rewards.

## Animated bodies

The BetterModel plugin draws and animates bot bodies listed in `appearance.animated-bodies`; players keep `BodyRig`, and a
server without the plugin uses `BodyRig` for everyone. `resourcepack/RigModels.py` turns the generated body and weapon models
into `resourcepack/bettermodel/*.bbmodel` with idle, walk, shoot, damage and death keyframes. Both read their joints from
`rig-bodies.yml`.

`appearance.motion` defines soldier, heavy, scout and medic walk and recoil profiles. `RigModels.py` reads the
bundled profiles for BetterModel clips; `BodyRig` reads them for fallback displays. Heavy units use shorter, slower steps,
scouts lean forward with a faster cycle, and medics use a brisk gait with mild recoil. Heroes keep their existing movement.
The fallback advances its cycle by distance, capped at the configured cadence, and settles at rest. Runtime overrides affect
fallback displays; changes to BetterModel profiles require regenerating the clips with `python3 resourcepack/RigModels.py`
(requires PyYAML). Animation-only changes leave the geometry intact and need no BetterModel resource-pack export.

The native bone routes already live in `resourcepack/src/assets/bettermodel`. `verifyBetterModel` checks that every
renderable rig bone has a route before packaging. A tracker has one glow for all viewers, so each body is two trackers on a plain and
an outlined copy of its model, each shown to the right viewers by the same rules as `TargetOutlines`. BetterModel loads its
models only once the server has loaded, so an animated body without one is logged then and stays a `BodyRig`.

## Changing a body

1. Edit what changes. Part shapes are in `resourcepack/SternenfrontArt.java`; joints, scale, arm lift and grip turn are in
   `src/main/resources/rig-bodies.yml`; walk and recoil are in `appearance.motion`. An animated body bakes its gun into
   its model, so `GUNS` in `resourcepack/RigModels.py` names it, and it must stay the class's blaster.
2. Run `./gradlew :resourcepack:generateRigModels`. It runs `SternenfrontArt.java`, then `RigModels.py` (requires PyYAML),
   which writes `resourcepack/bettermodel`.
3. An animation-only change needs the new `.bbmodel` files, with no texture export. For body geometry changes that the
   generator does not export, use `dev/verify/verify.py models` to refresh the native meshes from BetterModel.
4. `./gradlew build` validates every native bone route and packages the source assets. `stagePlugins` stages the packs
   and matching rig files without a runtime export merge.

## Unit colours

A side's fireteams wear the colours in `appearance.unit-colours` on their armour markings, first fireteam first, and a side
with more fireteams starts over. Players, heroes and reinforcements wear their side's first colour. A fireteam's leader
also wears pauldrons in its colour, which pass to whoever takes over the lead. A `BodyRig` puts the colour in a part's
second custom_model_data colour and the pauldrons behind its first flag; its hurt flash turns both colours red. An animated
body tints its `_marks` and `_pauldron` bones and toggles the pauldrons. BetterModel's flash restores each bone's tint
afterwards, so a new colour waits until the flash is over. Wherever nothing sets a colour, the pack shows the first one.

## Generated assets

Edit `resourcepack/SternenfrontArt.java` for bodies, hero bodies, helmets, blasters, sabers, vehicles, and effect
models. It writes the model JSON, item definitions, texture atlases, and class portraits. Editing its generated JSON
or PNG files directly loses the change on regeneration. Body parts must fit the joints in `rig-bodies.yml`.

`PackArt.java` turns the portraits into menu cards. `BattleArt.py` writes faction emblems, shield grids, and firing
poses. The Gradle dependencies run the body and equipment generator before both consumers:

```sh
./gradlew :resourcepack:generateTextures :resourcepack:generateBattle
```

Held and worn equipment uses `PackModel` and the vanilla item's custom-model-data override. Adding one requires
`:paper:api:packOverrides -PclientJar=/path/to/minecraft-client.jar`. Body parts, vehicles, and the wrist rocket
instead use direct resource keys such as `sternenfront/body/hero_jedi/torso`; keep those keys aligned with their
call sites. The large block-built ships belong to the saved world, while assault craft are display models.

Sound provenance and regeneration instructions live in [SABER_SOURCES.md](../../resourcepack/SABER_SOURCES.md) and
[BATTLE_AUDIO.md](../../resourcepack/BATTLE_AUDIO.md). The pack includes the [credits](../../CREDITS.md).

## Match intro

`intro` in `config.yml` controls the camera sequence before combat starts. The default lasts 180 ticks.
The round clock, bots, vehicles, objectives, and deployments start after the shared intro deadline.
Sneaking skips to the last shot but keeps the player waiting until that deadline.

`intro.shots` contains ordered keyframes. `at` is a fraction of the total duration, starting at `0` and ending at `1`.
`anchor` and `look-at` accept `overview`, `headquarters` for the viewer's faction, and zero-based `post:N`.
`offset` contains three world-space block coordinates relative to the anchor. `cut: true` holds the preceding
shot until the keyframe, then changes the camera immediately. Other segments use smooth interpolation. The default
post shot has a moving endpoint before the headquarters cut. The actor faces that shot's camera and starts its clip
when the first headquarters keyframe is reached. Explicit operator overrides of `intro.shots` retain their own path.

`intro.audio` controls the faction radio briefing, captions, and shot cues. `intro.audio.briefing` is on by default.
Disabling it leaves only the shot cues. Speech uses the client's Voice slider;
radio clicks and transitions use Ambient. Sounds follow the spectator camera and stop with the scene, including a skip.
The briefing starts three ticks after the opening fade. `briefing-ticks` reserves 48 ticks by default for speech before the closing
radio click. An intro too short for that window omits the briefing. Transitions follow the first intermediate camera
keyframe and the headquarters shot, outside the fades. Voice and effects volumes can be set independently.

The German transcripts are `intro.briefing` in `messages.yml`. Changing the words requires regenerating the speech with
`resourcepack/IntroBriefings.py --model /path/to/de_DE-thorsten-high.onnx`, using that script's Python dependencies.
The checked-in WAV sources allow offline OGG regeneration with `python3 resourcepack/IntroBriefings.py` and FFmpeg.
The engine and voice model remain build tools. [CREDITS.md](../../CREDITS.md#sound-effects) records the voice source.

`/sfintro` plays a personal preview in the lobby. It requires `blockcraft.sternenfront.presentation` and an applied
resource pack. Sneak or use `/sfintro stop` to end the preview. It restores the original position, game mode,
flight settings, invulnerability, collision setting, fall distance, and prior spectator target.
An external teleport cancels the scene without moving the player back to its starting position.

The shared `Scenes` API reports an `End` reason to its completion callback after restoring the player.
`SkipMode.COMPLETE` stops immediately and does not run remaining timeline cues. Story callers must apply their final
quest state in that callback for both `COMPLETED` and `SKIPPED`. Cancellation and disconnect require separate handling.
`SkipMode.HOLD`, used by the match intro, keeps cue timing and the shared deadline while showing the final camera pose.

## Infantry animation clips

The final intro shot shows an infantry actor using `animations/briefing.animation.json` from the plugin jar.
An operator override with the same relative path in the plugin data folder replaces it after restart.
The animation name is `animation.infantry.briefing`.
The bundled clip samples eased arm and head movement at 20 Hz. The salute rises over 0.55 seconds, holds until
1 second, and returns to rest by 1.5 seconds before the default shot fades. Its four-second loop has matching endpoints.

`BedrockAnimations` reads the numeric subset of Blockbench's Bedrock animation export. It supports positive
`animation_length`, boolean `loop`, and named bone `position`, `rotation`, and `scale` channels. A channel is a
constant three-number array or timestamped arrays. Timestamped objects can contain `post` and `lerp_mode: linear`.
Molang, curved interpolation, separate `pre` values, event tracks, and animation controllers are rejected.

The infantry actor binds `head`, `torso`, `left_arm`, `right_arm`, `left_leg`, and `right_leg` to existing item-display
parts. Position values are local offsets in sixteenths of a block. Rotation values are local XYZ degrees.
Scale values multiply each part. This actor uses independent joints with fixed attachment points and no parent-bone
inheritance. Model geometry and pivot placement remain in the existing Sternenfront model generator.
Unknown bone names reject the actor action. The importer does not load `.bbmodel` geometry or arbitrary Bedrock rigs.

## Check the intro in a client

1. Join the dev Sternenfront server with the current resource pack and the presentation permission.
2. Run `/sfintro`. Check the map flyover, faction-specific headquarters shot, letterboxing, fades, and the infantry salute.
   With `intro.audio.briefing` on, hear the opening radio click, the faction's German briefing and the closing click. Hear cues at the post and headquarters
   shots. Check the chat transcript and repeat for the other faction. Voice and Ambient sliders control separate sounds.
   The salute and head movements must ease into their holds and return without snapping.
3. Repeat the preview and sneak while the voice plays. The audio stops immediately. Check the original position, game
   mode, flight, and camera are restored. Repeat with `/sfintro stop`.
4. Repeat the preview and teleport to another world. Check the scene stops and the teleport destination is preserved.
5. Start a match with two clients. Skip on one client and watch the full intro on the other. Neither client can deploy early.
6. Check both clients deploy together while the final frame is black, then fade into the headquarters spawn.
   There must be no second fade-out, overview flash, or fall before deployment. The full configured round time remains.
   Bots and objectives begin at that point.
7. Disconnect during the intro and reconnect. Check the player can deploy when the shared intro ends.

The automated tests cover camera interpolation, cut timing, audio scheduling and cleanup, and numeric animation sampling
and validation. They do not verify camera smoothness, audio playback, shader appearance, model pivots, or client input.
