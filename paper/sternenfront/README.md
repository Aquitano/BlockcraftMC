# Sternenfront

The displayed Clone Wars mode retains the `sternenfront` plugin, server, permission, and storage identities.

## Maps

`BLOCKCRAFT_WORLD_TEMPLATE` lists the maps, and the server picks one each time it starts, so each round may play another.
Wrack von Tessar is built by hand. Sturmwerft, an offshore shipyard in a storm, is generated by `dev/mapgen/sturmwerft.py` and
`dev/verify/verify.py map`; change the script rather than the saved world. A map's `arena.yml` may leave out `secrets` and may
set `weather: rain` and a fixed `time`. On such a map `presentation.lightning` draws harmless strikes near a random fighter, and
real ones are cancelled. Keep its `distance` under spigot.yml's tracking range for other entities, or clients never see them.

Sturmwerft uses pale domes above a cold ocean, cyan-lit bridges, and a warship assembly dock. Deck scuffs follow the
routes between headquarters and command posts. Maintenance carts, opened machinery, pipes, and replacement panels sit
beside those routes. `environment.steam` and `environment.sparks` in its generated arena metadata locate the ambient
effects. `environment.crane.from` and `to` define the overhead trolley's rail. Its suspended load uses six non-colliding
block displays and is removed at round end. `presentation.shipyard` controls particle counts, range, sound volume, and
crane timing. These effects start with combat and stay off on maps without the corresponding metadata.
Its `world-border-size` includes the offshore scenery while `play-area` keeps the combat and replay bounds.

Each entry under `secrets` is optional. Tessar keeps `crystals`, `droid-path`, `dune`, `music-box`, and `course`.
A shipyard can supply only `receiver: "x y z"` or `inspection-course`. The latter has `lane` as
`[minX, minY, minZ, maxX, maxY, maxZ]`, `pit-y` as a number, `gates` as ordered X planes from start to finish,
and `board: "x y z"`. Gates can run west to east or east to west. The inspection course uses fixed physical platforms;
the moving crane's displays provide no collision.

The receiver consumes right clicks before weapons, plays the original cantina tune and grants `sternenfront.receiver`
once through `Discoveries` and `UnlockRewards`. Its German transmission appears in chat so the combat HUD cannot replace it.
Only deployed players on foot can activate it.
The inspection course reuses `KesselRun` and grants `sternenfront.inspection` on the first valid completion.
`secrets.inspection-course.par-seconds` sets its timing goal; `limit-seconds` ends an attempt.
Its board shows the five fastest times in the current round and never reads or writes `bc_sternenfront_kessel`.
Mounting, teleporting, death, leaving the lane or falling below `pit-y` interrupts an attempt.
Round end and plugin disable remove secret displays and stop playback and tasks.

## Compatibility

`kolonien` identifies the Republic and `hegemonie` the Separatists in arena metadata, configuration, and player
settings. The hero keys `jedi`, `commander`, `sith`, and `bounty-hunter` identify Obi-Wan, Rex, Dooku, and Jango.
Changing display names does not rename those persisted keys.

## Reinforcement units

The deploy menu offers one reinforcement per faction, bought with the same battle points as heroes for one life.
The Republic's ARC trooper costs 250 points, has 32 health, and carries a cooler-firing support repeater with shield wall and adrenaline.
The Separatists' BX commando droid costs 300 points, has 26 health and 112% movement speed, and carries a short-range carbine with jump pack and grenade.
Each faction can field two reinforcements alongside its named hero slots. `reinforcement-units` configures each unit's cost, limit, health, and speed.
`blasters.arc-repeater` and `blasters.commando-carbine` configure their weapons. The abilities use the existing `abilities` settings.

Selection reserves nothing. Choosing a spawn confirms the purchase; changing the paid role requires confirmation again.
Automatic deployment without confirmation uses the free class. Deployment checks the balance and capacity again, and a failed deployment restores the points and slot.
If the unit is unavailable, the player deploys as their free class. Death, disconnect, and round end release the unit.
Taking the relic also releases the unit and replaces its loadout with the champion's. Reinforcements receive normal healing and normal kill rewards.
They have no permanent unlock or database record. Their body rigs and helmets come from `resourcepack/SternenfrontArt.java`.

## Bots

Bots fill each side to `bots.fill-per-team` fighters, players included, and fight in fireteams of up to
`bots.squad.fireteam-size`, led by a soldier where the team has one. Each new bot takes the class its side has fewest of.
A team shares one objective: a post it can still take, or a held post with enemies in it, keeping its choice unless another
is `switch-margin` blocks closer. A held post under attack counts `reinforce-pull` blocks closer, enough to bring a team
back from the neighbouring post but not across the map. When a team moves on from a post it took, it leaves its heavy,
else a soldier or scout other than its leader, as the post's guard; it keeps one guard out at most. The guard moves
between the post's lookouts and rejoins the team when the post falls. Once every post is held, teams hold the one nearest
the enemy. A team at a post its side holds keeps off the capture point, which only a guard holds: it pushes attackers it
knows of, or enemies standing in the post, and otherwise takes the post's lookouts nearest the enemy, facing them. A team near a player of its faction takes that player's order. A team coming from
another post joins the new route nearest its inner end rather than walking back to its outer waypoints.

On the move the others walk in the leader's steps, `spacing` blocks apart, each `stagger` blocks to alternate sides where the
ground allows. A member keeps the leader's pace once in its place and runs only when it falls a few places behind. The
leader slows for a fit member behind it, and a member ahead waits for the column. Under fire the team bounds
by pairs along its authored route: one pair runs `bound-distance` blocks while the other covers it from cover and fires at
where the enemy was last seen. A team held up for `flank-after-ticks` sends one member round by another route. Near a
post it is taking the whole team spreads into it. Bots take cover only while covering, venting or wounded.

A bot below 30% health leaves the push until it heals past that: it takes cover, else runs to its fireteam's medic, else
falls back from the enemy, else stands still. A wounded leader hands the lead to a fit member. Bots regenerate out of
combat by the same `regen` rule as players, and medics heal bots as they heal players.

A team whose leader has twice as many enemies as friends within `fall-back-range`, and at least two, runs back to its
route's retreat points. It pushes again as soon as it is back there and no longer outnumbered, and waits there
`fall-back-ticks` at most. Odds still bad by then, or bad again within `fall-back-ticks` of pushing, make it give the post
up for another objective. A leader left alone in a fight falls back once the same way to wait for its teammates. A guard
whose post is that outnumbered rejoins its team, and a guard that stays takes cover first when it is wounded or venting.
A bot that stands within 1.5 blocks of one spot for `stand-ticks` while it knows where an enemy is runs there for five
seconds: an enemy it sees, one it saw recently, or its team's contact under fire, never one hidden behind a wall that nobody
spotted. A press that leaves the bot where it stood gives up until it moves again. Wounded bots, medics treating a patient, bots falling back, heavies in siege mode and guards at a lookout stay put.
When a member falls, its teammates take the killer's position as contact for
`alarm-ticks`; a bot with nothing in sight turns toward enemy fire within `hearing-range`. Soldiers and heavies throw their
explosives at enemies dug into a post or hidden behind cover, and a heavy holding open ground under fire raises its shield
wall. A bot's aim scatters on a fresh target and settles over `aim-settle-ticks`, and its first shots go to one side
of the target, closing in over the same ticks. It opens up while the bot walks and as its blaster heats, by the same
`combat.handling` rule as a player's. Each member watches its own sector.

## Arena template

A map's template is restored before each server start. Positions and bot routes come from
`blockcraft/Sternenfront/arena.yml` inside that template. A map edit needs both the saved blocks and updated metadata.

- A post's listed colour blocks must remain recolourable. The round replaces their material for the current owner.
- Door panels start as air. The round opens any panels left closed in a saved template.
- Landing grounds lie at x -22 and x 22 beside each post. Each needs a flat 7-by-7 surface, open sky, and 6.5 blocks
  of clearance around its centre for the assault craft.
- A vehicle two blocks wide that steps one block must reach every landing ground from the headquarters pads.
- Replays include the play area. The far mesas outside it are scenery and are absent from a replay.

## Client constraints

Body rigs are cosmetic. Combat uses the underlying fighter's hitbox, including the larger-looking B2. Fighters see
other deployed bodies as rigs; the owner keeps their normal first-person and F5 body. Spectators see the original
entities of rigged bodies so the kill camera remains usable, and the plain model of animated bots. Hiding a player's body
also removes them from that viewer's tab list.

A recording holds the bare fighters. `RecordedModel` marks a bot with its model, so a replay server with BetterModel draws
and animates it again; without the plugin it plays back as an equipped skeleton. Players play back as mannequins.

Scope zoom uses movement slowdown. Clients with FOV effects disabled see the slowdown without a narrower view.
Music and cantina audio use the Music category. Combat effects use the Players category.

Launched rockets and orbital strikes continue after their owner falls. Lightning channels end with the caster.
Both powers use the existing damage path for spawn protection and kill credit. Rockets also use `blastAround` for
cover, teammate safety, and self-damage. Lightning uses the saber guard without reflecting the attack.

## Redeployment

The middle toolbar button confirms a quick deployment with the selected class, hero, or reinforcement. It queues the deployment during
the five-second wait. Without confirmation, the fifteen-second timer deploys the existing free class without spending battle points.
Selecting a different paid unit requires another confirmation, including when a spawn was already queued.
The spawn card distinguishes a pending choice from a confirmed deployment. A failed teleport clears confirmation and
restarts the automatic countdown, leaving the menu open for another choice.

The suggestion keeps an explicitly chosen spawn for the current deployment if it is still safe. Outside an assault,
it remembers a safe post selected by clicking its spawn card. HQ is never a lasting preference, and the assault priority
takes precedence over remembered posts. Otherwise it prefers a safe owned post closer to the objective, with HQ as the fallback.
Quick deployment does not save its suggestion as a preference. The actual deployment rechecks ownership
and live enemy presence after the fade; a lost or threatened post is replaced by another safe recommendation.
A post also needs a spawn at least `respawn.enemy-clearance` blocks from every enemy, by default 12 blocks.
The dropship remains an explicit choice and is never recommended automatically.

The right toolbar button opens a tactical schematic with north up and east right. It projects the current map's post and
HQ positions into inventory slots, preserving scale and separating overlapping markers. Colours show ownership.
Posts A–E use the same letter badges as the combat HUD in both deployment views. Filled badges allow deployment, outlines mark locked posts,
and blinking badges mark contested or attacked posts. In both views the pack frames the chosen spawn on its slot's border:
gold corners while it waits for a confirmation, a green frame and a glow once confirmed. A star marks the recommended
spawn and a padlock a locked one. The spawn the player will land at shows the seconds left as its count: the wait once
confirmed, otherwise the automatic deployment. The confirmation button names its target and shows the wait or automatic
deployment countdown. Hovering shows the post name, recommendation, selection, and for a locked spawn `Gesperrt` with
the reason. The landing button names its target
post. The same button returns to the class cards. The faction's reinforcement card sits beside its hero cards.

After spawning, the minimap frames the post to make for in yellow corners: the assault's priority post, or the nearest
objective outside the assault. When all posts are safe, it frames the post nearest the enemy HQ for defence.

## Minimap

A deployed player with the pack sees a map in the top-left corner, north up and centred on them, `minimap.radius` blocks
to its rim. It shows the posts with the combat HUD's letter badges and ownership colours, both headquarters, the player's
arrow at the nearest of sixteen headings, their squad as gold dots and other allies as smaller dots of their side's colour.
Posts beyond the radius stay on the rim along their bearing. Yellow corners frame the post to make for, as Redeployment describes. Headquarters and fighters beyond it are left out, so
nothing on the rim hides a post's badge. An enemy shows for `minimap.enemy-ticks` after each shot it fires, and only
while the player's client draws it, so a vanished player or a hidden body never shows. The list under the map names the
player and their squad beside class marks, in the menu tiles' capitals, and dims anyone dead or choosing a spawn.
`minimap.bot` in `messages.yml` names the bots.

A player's squad is the fireteam that last took their order (see Bots). It stays theirs while no other team takes it,
and passes to the next team that does once it follows someone else or nobody. Other players with the same squad are listed.

The map is a boss bar line of its own that the pack's text shader moves into the corner, and it hides with the combat
HUD for dialogues, screen effects and scenes. Its slot comes after the objective line, so a post's capture bar or a
hero's bar stands one slot lower than without it. It redraws every `minimap.update-ticks` and sends nothing while
nothing on it moved.

## Combat feedback

During combat, enemies have red outlines with every weapon, without aiming or sneaking. This applies to players and
bots, including their custom body parts. Outlines are private to the viewer. `outlines.enabled`, `outlines.range` and
`outlines.allies`, which adds cyan outlines for allies, control them. The applied resource pack clips outline pixels against the
world depth buffer, so walls hide the covered parts while exposed parts stay outlined. Selection uses no server-side
block rays. Outlines stay disabled until the pack has loaded.

Each shot's sound is pitched up to 6% higher or lower at random. A player's shots carry 32 blocks and a bot's 24. The
shooter hears their own shot at full volume, and it follows them as they move. A bolt that hits a fighter or a wall
plays an impact sound. A hit body flashes `appearance.hurt-tint` for ten ticks, and an animated bot body also flinches.
A hit bot clanks if it is a droid and grunts if it is a clone, at most once per `bots.hurt-sound.cooldown-ticks`.

Hits, headshots, and kills have distinct sounds and immediate marks around the crosshair; a headshot's has a diamond above it. A kill replaces a weaker
marker even within the same tick. A red arc around the crosshair points at whoever last hurt the player, for
`combat.damage-indicator-ticks`, and follows as they turn. While a player with the pack holds a blaster that has spread,
four ticks around the crosshair show it. They open one step each time the spread, worked out as for the next shot, passes
a multiple of that blaster's base in `combat-feedback.spread-steps`, so every blaster closes standing still, crouched or
cool and opens fully hot on the move. A scoped or spreadless long rifle shows none, and the vanilla crosshair stays inside
the ticks. All three share the title's subtitle, centred with `UiFont`.
Kill and assist popups appear briefly beside the heat or guard meter.
A player with the pack sees their own kills, assists and death in the top-right corner, newest on top, at most four
lines. A line reads killer, mark and victim in the minimap's capitals, with the player's own name in white and others
in their side's colour: a skull for a kill or a death, its outline for an assist and a diamond beside it for a headshot.
Bots go by their minimap call signs. A line greys out in its last second and is gone after `kill-feed.seconds`. It rides
on the minimap's boss bar line, and stays up while the player is dead so the kill cam shows who it was. Sternenfront's
own potion effects hide their icons, which would otherwise cover the feed.
A grenade holds `abilities.grenade.charges` throws, full on each deployment, and they come back one at a time, each
`cooldown-seconds` after the one before. The HUD shows the throws left as pips and the seconds to the next only when none is
left, which is also the only time the vanilla cooldown runs on its material. Bots keep a plain cooldown.
Hero and elite availability appear only for `combat-feedback.hero-ready-ticks` and `combat-feedback.elite-ready-ticks`;
idle kill counts and point progress stay hidden.
Nearby gains combine into one segment.
A player with the pack sees their abilities as a column of tiles in the screen's bottom-right corner: F against the
corner, Q above it, then a bladed hero's leap under a double-jump badge, and a kill streak's strike framed in gold under
Shift+F until it is thrown. A key with nothing bound shows no tile. Each tile carries its key on a badge and the
ability's full name beside it. A ready tile is bright and framed in the side's colour; a recharging one turns grey
behind the seconds left and a fill that rises as it recharges. Several uses show as pips under the name. The tiles ride
on the action bar: `CombatHud` puts them in the line's middle, which needs the line's width as the client measures it,
and the pack's text shader moves them into the corner. Feedback that replaces the action bar, such as an ability's
cooldown notice, hides them until it ends. `Kühlt` means cooling. A loadout change
explains each binding in chat with its key, full name and what it does.
`ObjectiveHud` shows reinforcements, a tile per post and the time left in a boss bar's text line, and the pack draws that colour and overlay of bar as nothing. The screen's
edges redden with a hit and while health is low. `combat.handling` tunes spread and damage falloff.
Spread opens on the move and more in the air, and `combat.handling.blasters` sets those factors per blaster, so pistols
stay steadier walking and the repeater wants a crouch. A sprint shows the lowered pose of the held blaster, which cannot
fire until its `sprint-recovery-ticks` after the sprint ends. The crosshair ticks use the same factors as the shot.
A title's subtitle stays readable under the marks, which `UiFont.centredOn` keeps on the crosshair however long the line is. The sidebar stays hidden during a fight.

The heat meter turns red and shows `HEISS` at `heat.warning-level`, or when the next shot would overheat the weapon.
Venting shows `Kühlt` until the weapon is ready, followed by a ready sound. A vent also brackets `heat.window-share` of the
meter in cyan, somewhere in its lower half, and the meter then lights every cell the cooling heat has not left. The first
fresh pull of the trigger while the last lit cell is inside the bracket ends the vent with a click and steam; one outside
it adds `heat.miss-penalty-share` of the vent and closes the window. A trigger held since the overheating shot counts for
neither, and bots never try. `combat-feedback` tunes marker and
popup durations and the hit, headshot, and kill volumes.
The vents on every blaster show its heat to its holder and everyone else: grey when cold, orange from `heat.glow.warm`,
bright from `heat.warning-level`, and pulsing while venting. The colour is the held item's first custom_model_data
colour, set with the firing pose's model string, so the item changes only when the step or the pose does. The client
does not lower the gun for these changes, because the hoe keeps its item type and its override sets
`hand_animation_on_swap` to false. Animated bot bodies leave the vents out, because their rig has no item to tint them;
the display bodies show them.

A player's bolts and long-rifle shots meet fighters, speeders and heads where that player's screen showed them, which is
their ping plus the client's smoothing of other entities behind the server. Each tick records every target's hitbox for that.
Walls, shields, guard and parry stay in the present, and bots' shots see only the present. `combat.lag-compensation` turns
it off or caps how far back it reaches.

Left click sweeps a saber-colored slash through every enemy ahead, including when aimed at air. Colored particles
follow a continuous white line with a solid core visible from the side. Wall-clipped hit rays position its segments,
each lasting `presentation.saber-trail-ticks` under the shared effect cap. Each enemy can take damage or block only once per swing.
`combat.melee` sets the reach, arc angle, cooldown and animation duration. Defaults are 3.5 blocks, 120 degrees,
12 ticks between swings and a four-tick sweep. Successive swings alternate direction. Switching weapons or guarding
cancels the remaining sweep. The relic blade uses the same attack.
A saber's hum, ignition, retraction and swings and the raised guard play on the hero, so they follow them as they move.
A viewer who sees the hero's body rig hears them on its torso, because a client drops a sound on an entity it does not
draw. The thrown saber flies flat and spins about its middle like a disc, whirling every five ticks.
Other fighters see a bladed hero's body rig hold the blade tipped forward, bring both hands in with the blade across the
chest while guarding, wind up on the side a swing starts from and follow through over the sweep, and thrust the off hand
forward while a lightning channel or grip lasts. A Force push first draws that hand back for `wind-up-ticks` while air
gathers at the palm and a rising whoosh plays, then thrusts it forward as the push lands. Force lightning leaves that
hand as forked bolts redrawn every tick in a new shape every second tick, while its damage still lands on the channel's
pulses. Display interpolation eases each change over a few ticks. In first person the guard rolls the blade across the
view through the item's `using_item` model.

## What keeps a player going

Within a life, kills chain: each one within `combat-feedback.multikill-seconds` of the last adds `heroes.points.multikill` and a
higher chime, and a killing headshot adds `heroes.points.headshot`. Both scale with what the kill pays, so bots pay less than
players. A bot kill counts towards stats, pays `rewards.bot-kill` and fills the orbital strike's streak at `streaks.bots-per-kill`.
A kill by something still flying after its owner fell pays as usual but does not count towards the next life's streak.

Battle points also feed the round's awards, which need two fighters and pay `rewards.award`, and a rank that lasts across rounds:
`bc_sternenfront_career` keeps every player's total and best round, `career.ranks` sets the thresholds and `career.rank.<index>`
in `messages.yml` names them. The details button of the round recap in chat adds battle points and captured posts to the
shared results and rewards.

## Animated bodies

The BetterModel plugin draws and animates bot bodies listed in `appearance.animated-bodies`; players keep `BodyRig`, and a
server without the plugin uses `BodyRig` for everyone. `resourcepack/RigModels.py` turns the generated body and weapon models
into `resourcepack/bettermodel/*.bbmodel` with idle, walk, shoot, damage and death keyframes. Its joints must match `RigBody`.

`appearance.motion` defines soldier, heavy, scout and medic walk and recoil profiles. `RigModels.py` reads the
bundled profiles for BetterModel clips; `BodyRig` reads them for fallback displays. Heavy units use shorter, slower steps,
scouts lean forward with a faster cycle, and medics use a brisk gait with mild recoil. Heroes keep their existing movement.
The fallback advances its cycle by distance, capped at the configured cadence, and settles at rest. Runtime overrides affect
fallback displays; changes to BetterModel profiles require regenerating the clips with `python3 resourcepack/RigModels.py`
(requires PyYAML). Animation-only changes leave the geometry intact and need no BetterModel resource-pack export.

BetterModel builds the pack from those files on the game server, and that output is committed in
`resourcepack/src/assets/bettermodel`. After changing a body, run `SternenfrontArt.java`, then `dev/verify/verify.py models`,
which needs the verify network running. A tracker has one glow for all viewers, so each body is two trackers on a plain and
an outlined copy of its model, each shown to the right viewers by the same rules as `TargetOutlines`.

## Unit colours

A side's fireteams wear the colours in `appearance.unit-colours` on their armour markings, first fireteam first, and a side
with more fireteams starts over. Players, heroes and reinforcements wear their side's first colour. A fireteam's leader
also wears pauldrons in its colour, which pass to whoever takes over the lead. A `BodyRig` puts the colour in a part's
second custom_model_data colour and the pauldrons behind its first flag; its hurt flash turns both colours red. An animated
body tints its `_marks` and `_pauldron` bones and toggles the pauldrons. BetterModel's flash restores each bone's tint
afterwards, so a new colour waits until the flash is over. Wherever nothing sets a colour, the pack shows the first one.

## Generated assets

Edit `resourcepack/SternenfrontArt.java` for bodies, hero bodies, helmets, blasters, sabers, vehicles, and effect
models. It writes the model JSON, item definitions, texture atlases, and class portraits. Editing its generated JSON
or PNG files directly loses the change on regeneration. Body dimensions and pivots must agree with `RigBody`.

`PackArt.java` turns the portraits into menu cards. `BattleArt.py` writes faction emblems, shield grids, and firing
poses. The Gradle dependencies run the body and equipment generator before both consumers:

```sh
./gradlew :resourcepack:generateTextures :resourcepack:generateBattle
```

Held and worn equipment uses `PackModel` and the vanilla item's custom-model-data override. Adding one requires
`:paper:api:packOverrides -PclientJar=/path/to/minecraft-client.jar`. Body parts, vehicles, and the wrist rocket
instead use direct resource keys such as `sternenfront/body/hero_jedi/torso`; keep those keys aligned with their
call sites. The large block-built ships belong to the saved world, while assault craft are display models.

Sound provenance and regeneration instructions live in [SABER_SOURCES.md](../../resourcepack/SABER_SOURCES.md) and
[BATTLE_AUDIO.md](../../resourcepack/BATTLE_AUDIO.md). The pack includes the [credits](../../CREDITS.md).

## Match intro

`intro` in `config.yml` controls the camera sequence before combat starts. The default lasts 180 ticks.
The round clock, bots, vehicles, objectives, and deployments start after the shared intro deadline.
Sneaking skips to the last shot but keeps the player waiting until that deadline.

`intro.shots` contains ordered keyframes. `at` is a fraction of the total duration, starting at `0` and ending at `1`.
`anchor` and `look-at` accept `overview`, `headquarters` for the viewer's faction, and zero-based `post:N`.
`offset` contains three world-space block coordinates relative to the anchor. `cut: true` holds the preceding
shot until the keyframe, then changes the camera immediately. Other segments use smooth interpolation. The default
post shot has a moving endpoint before the headquarters cut. The actor faces that shot's camera and starts its clip
when the first headquarters keyframe is reached. Explicit operator overrides of `intro.shots` retain their own path.

`intro.audio` controls the faction radio briefing, captions, and shot cues. `intro.audio.briefing` is on by default.
Disabling it leaves only the shot cues. Speech uses the client's Voice slider;
radio clicks and transitions use Ambient. Sounds follow the spectator camera and stop with the scene, including a skip.
The briefing starts three ticks after the opening fade. `briefing-ticks` reserves 48 ticks by default for speech before the closing
radio click. An intro too short for that window omits the briefing. Transitions follow the first intermediate camera
keyframe and the headquarters shot, outside the fades. Voice and effects volumes can be set independently.

The German transcripts are `intro.briefing` in `messages.yml`. Changing the words requires regenerating the speech with
`resourcepack/IntroBriefings.py --model /path/to/de_DE-thorsten-high.onnx`, using that script's Python dependencies.
The checked-in WAV sources allow offline OGG regeneration with `python3 resourcepack/IntroBriefings.py` and FFmpeg.
The engine and voice model remain build tools. [CREDITS.md](../../CREDITS.md#sound-effects) records the voice source.

`/sfintro` plays a personal preview in the lobby. It requires `blockcraft.sternenfront.presentation` and an applied
resource pack. Sneak or use `/sfintro stop` to end the preview. It restores the original position, game mode,
flight settings, invulnerability, collision setting, fall distance, and prior spectator target.
An external teleport cancels the scene without moving the player back to its starting position.

The shared `Scenes` API reports an `End` reason to its completion callback after restoring the player.
`SkipMode.COMPLETE` stops immediately and does not run remaining timeline cues. Story callers must apply their final
quest state in that callback for both `COMPLETED` and `SKIPPED`. Cancellation and disconnect require separate handling.
`SkipMode.HOLD`, used by the match intro, keeps cue timing and the shared deadline while showing the final camera pose.

## Infantry animation clips

The final intro shot shows an infantry actor using `animations/briefing.animation.json` from the plugin jar.
An operator override with the same relative path in the plugin data folder replaces it after restart.
The animation name is `animation.infantry.briefing`.
The bundled clip samples eased arm and head movement at 20 Hz. The salute rises over 0.55 seconds, holds until
1 second, and returns to rest by 1.5 seconds before the default shot fades. Its four-second loop has matching endpoints.

`BedrockAnimations` reads the numeric subset of Blockbench's Bedrock animation export. It supports positive
`animation_length`, boolean `loop`, and named bone `position`, `rotation`, and `scale` channels. A channel is a
constant three-number array or timestamped arrays. Timestamped objects can contain `post` and `lerp_mode: linear`.
Molang, curved interpolation, separate `pre` values, event tracks, and animation controllers are rejected.

The infantry actor binds `head`, `torso`, `left_arm`, `right_arm`, `left_leg`, and `right_leg` to existing item-display
parts. Position values are local offsets in sixteenths of a block. Rotation values are local XYZ degrees.
Scale values multiply each part. This actor uses independent joints with fixed attachment points and no parent-bone
inheritance. Model geometry and pivot placement remain in the existing Sternenfront model generator.
Unknown bone names reject the actor action. The importer does not load `.bbmodel` geometry or arbitrary Bedrock rigs.

## Check the intro in a client

1. Join the dev Sternenfront server with the current resource pack and the presentation permission.
2. Run `/sfintro`. Check the map flyover, faction-specific headquarters shot, letterboxing, fades, and the infantry salute.
   With `intro.audio.briefing` on, hear the opening radio click, the faction's German briefing and the closing click. Hear cues at the post and headquarters
   shots. Check the chat transcript and repeat for the other faction. Voice and Ambient sliders control separate sounds.
   The salute and head movements must ease into their holds and return without snapping.
3. Repeat the preview and sneak while the voice plays. The audio stops immediately. Check the original position, game
   mode, flight, and camera are restored. Repeat with `/sfintro stop`.
4. Repeat the preview and teleport to another world. Check the scene stops and the teleport destination is preserved.
5. Start a match with two clients. Skip on one client and watch the full intro on the other. Neither client can deploy early.
6. Check both clients deploy together while the final frame is black, then fade into the headquarters spawn.
   There must be no second fade-out, overview flash, or fall before deployment. The full configured round time remains.
   Bots and objectives begin at that point.
7. Disconnect during the intro and reconnect. Check the player can deploy when the shared intro ends.

The automated tests cover camera interpolation, cut timing, audio scheduling and cleanup, and numeric animation sampling
and validation. They do not verify camera smoothness, audio playback, shader appearance, model pivots, or client input.
